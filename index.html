Nothing here is decided. Some ideas are from Smári, some are from Claude (the AI assistant Smári works with), and the empty spot at the end is yours.

A friendlier calendar

Make the calendar something students actually enjoy opening.

Google Calendar is a good place to look for inspiration: moving between day, week and month, creating and dragging an event, seeing a lot at a glance.

See the research

Better group projects

Make the group project part of the site nicer and easier to work with.

For example with a peer evaluation process like the one Sustainable Island has now, where teammates give each other feedback as part of the project.

See the research

A theme for every module

The whole site changes its look when a new module starts.

Each module gets its own visual theme, and the site switches automatically on the day the calendar says the module begins. A design module should not look like a back-end module.

See the research

How does the school get more students?

A research project with real consequences.

What should the school do to reach more people? SEO, so search engines find us. GEO, so AI assistants recommend us. Or a change of direction: AI-driven web development, Forward Deployed Engineer, something else entirely.

See the research

An app with push notifications

Turn the website into an app that taps students on the shoulder.

Students should notice what is happening in the calendar without having to remember to look.

See the research

The student just ahead of you

Gamification, without the leaderboard that makes people give up.

Points for returned guides, reviews given and grades. But each student only sees the one person just above them and the one just below. You want to catch the one ahead and stay in front of the one behind, and nobody feels bad about someone far beyond them.

See the research

Our own streaming platform

Lectures, screen sharing and recordings on our own site.

Today we depend on Zoom for teaching. What if the classroom lived on our own site instead, next to the calendar and the guides, built the way we want it?

See the research

A way for companies to find our students

You are building the portfolios. This is the other side of them.

One page where a company can browse the students who are open to internships, filter by what they are good at, and get in touch. A portfolio helps once someone is looking at you. This gets them looking.

See the research

The first week on the site

A new student meets guides, reviews, grades, calendar and groups all at once.

What should day one feel like? A short tour, a first small task that teaches how returning and reviewing works, a clear picture of how grades are built. You were new here not long ago, so you remember what was confusing.

See the research

Your term, looked back on

A personal page at the end of each term that is worth sharing.

What you built, the guides you returned, the reviews you gave, the best feedback you got, how far you have come. It compares you with yourself a few months ago instead of with other students.

See the research

Watch real students use it, then fix it

Find the problems nobody on the team can see any more.

Sit with a few students while they use the site on their own phone and laptop. Write down every place they get stuck. Try it with only a keyboard, and with a screen reader. Then fix the worst things and show the before and after.

+ Yours This spot is empty on purpose. Half an idea is enough to fill it.

Got one? Put it on the wall.

Everyone pushes straight to main. No branches, no pull requests, nobody approves anything. A minute after you push, your idea is on this page.

  1. Get the repository

    git clone https://github.com/ellertsmari/Internship-ideas.git
cd Internship-ideas

  2. Add your card

    Open index.html, search for YOUR IDEA GOES ABOVE THIS LINE, and paste this just above it:

    <article class="idea" data-from="Your name">
  <h3>The name of your idea</h3>
  <p class="pitch">One sentence that makes people curious.</p>
  <p>Anything else you want to say.</p>
  <ul class="notes">
  </ul>
</article>

    Open index.html in your browser to see how it looks. There is nothing to install and nothing to build.

  3. Push it

    git add index.html
git commit -m "Add my idea"
git pull --rebase
git push

    If git reports a conflict, someone added an idea at the same moment. Keep both cards.

Want the full research page too?

Every idea on the wall has a page behind it with research, links and a way to start. Yours can have one as well. Open the repository in your AI coding assistant, put your idea in the first line of this prompt, and let it do the digging. Then read what it wrote, fix what is wrong, and push.

Prefer to write it yourself? Copy ideas/_template.html and fill it in. 

My idea for our next internship project at Vefskólinn: [DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA IN A SENTENCE OR TWO]. My name is [YOUR NAME].

This repository is a small static site that collects project ideas. Before you do anything, read index.html, ideas/_template.html and one finished page, for example ideas/streaming.html. If my idea is unclear, ask me questions first.

Then:

1. Research my idea properly on the web. What is already known about it? Who has done something similar, and what can we learn from them? How could it be built, and what could go wrong? Only use facts you found in a real source, and check that every link works.

2. Write a research page for it as ideas/[short-name].html. Follow ideas/_template.html exactly and write in the same plain, direct voice as the other pages. Pick one of the colours c1 to c7 for the page.

3. Add a card for it in index.html, just above the line that says YOUR IDEA GOES ABOVE THIS LINE, with my name in data-from and a "See the research" link to the new page.

4. When you are done, tell me what you could not verify, what you are unsure about, and which three findings surprised you most.

Do not change style.css, script.js or anybody else's idea. Do not commit or push. I will read it first.

Not at your computer? Edit index.html directly on GitHub and commit from there.

Or build on someone else's.

Like an idea? Think it should be twice as big, or half? See a problem with it? Say so, right on the card. That goes for Smári's and Claude's ideas as much as anyone's.

Find the idea in index.html and add a line inside its <ul class="notes">:

<li><b>Your name:</b> What you think, or how you would change it.</li>

Then commit and push, the same way as above.

  • Half an idea is enough. One sentence is a good start. Someone else may have the other half.
  • Nothing is too big, too small or too strange. Big ideas can shrink and small ones can grow.
  • It doesn't have to be code. Research, design, user testing and content are real projects.
  • Put your name on it. So we know who to ask.
